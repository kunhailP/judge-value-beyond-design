# Pre-submission revision (2026-10-09)

Response to the pre-submission review (overall ≈ 3.5, three requests) and the follow-up list of what would move it to 4.

## 1. Implementation vs description of the weighted design (fixed: description)
* Verified: `110_unit_audit.py` bins the positive dissimilarities into three groups (below the median, Q3, Q4), not
  four quartiles. Behaviour since lock v0.8 (`a6ba3a3`); no lock specifies the number of bins; only the manuscript was wrong.
* Decision: keep the locked three-bin behaviour as the default (every locked and exploratory run used it), describe it
  correctly (Setup → Designs), record the correction in `prereg/LOCK_HISTORY.md`, and report a `--bins 4` variant as a
  sensitivity check: locked cells (`results/LOCKED_VARIANTS.md`) and the 30 decisions (`results/VARIANTS.md`).
* Outcome in the locked cells: weighted J50 changes by ≤ 0.8 %, the best fixed design's saving by ≤ 0.1 points.

## 2. Same-design comparison in Table 1 (fixed)
* Lock v1.1 applied every metric on the weighted design; on zh→en the cheapest judge-free design is dedup. Table 1 now
  shows every evaluator on the cell's own best fixed design (run tag `_mtmex`: the locked run plus `--extra` arms, same
  seed; the locked arms reproduce the committed records exactly). zh→en ε = 0.01: best metric HES +7.3 % [−6, 19] on
  dedup (was −0.9 % on weighted); ε = 0.02: +1.3 % (was +0.8 %). H8 unchanged (4/4). Figure 2 uses the same numbers.
* The locked H8 numbers remain in `results/MTME_VERDICTS.md`; the appendix states both.

## 3. PPSR positioning (rewritten)
* Introduction: PPSR is itself a realised, resampling-verified saving (≈ 9–15 % for the top WMT22 metrics under uniform
  labelling and a coefficient fitted on the evaluation labels). The paper's question is what remains of it for a local
  decision once a judge-free design is credited first and the coefficient must be fitted on a pilot. Contributions
  re-ordered: design-first decomposition (incl. the top-10 evaluator result) → what is ruled out → levels → cost law.
* Related work: PPSR answers which evaluator saves most; we ask whether the saving is worth having; its ρ² is the first
  factor of our cost law.

## 4. From "no significant gain" to "how large a gain is ruled out" (new: `code/125_exclusion.py`)
* Per (decision, ε, evaluator): one-sided 95 % bootstrap upper bound of HES over the matched base; share below 5 % / 10 %;
  savings in labels; per-cell interval of the MEAN evaluator (all / top-10) and of design − mean evaluator.
* The 30 decisions re-run with 2,000 simulated audits per cell (`RUN_PAIRS_EXTRA.sh`, tag `n2000`): interval half-width
  6.6 → 2.3 points; 36 informative cells; design median 4.6 %, mean evaluator 0.2 / 2.3 / 0.9 % (pilot / refit / xfit);
  refitted upper bound < 5 % in 64 % and < 10 % in 94 % of 1,116 pairs (top-10: 29 % / 88 %); mean evaluator per cell
  < 10 % in 97 % of cells (one exception: en→de 24, ε = 0.01). 53 % of refitted pairs are now significantly > 0 — small
  and real (median 2.0 %). Labels: median decision needs 246; design saves 17, mean evaluator 6, best 17.

## 5. Small pilots (new: tags `p25`, `p10`)
* The 30 decisions with pilots of 25 and 10 segments, same menus/ε/rules: 49 and 60 informative cells, pilot share
  27 % / 13 %, design 4.3 % / 4.4 %, mean evaluator refit 0.7 % / 0.3 %, pilot-fixed −2.7 % / −7.7 %, design wins
  86 % / 90 % (refit). The frozen-coefficient tax grows as 1/P_eff, as the cost law predicts.
