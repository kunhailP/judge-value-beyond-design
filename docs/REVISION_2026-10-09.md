## Third pass (2026-10-10): known-zero differences and the oracle coefficient
* Semi-synthetic judges drew independent noise per output, so items whose every competitor output equals the candidate's
  (102 of 1,315 en→de, 35 of 1,875 zh→en segments on the top-4 menus; never sampled under dedup/weighted/active) carried a
  non-zero prediction term (1/N) Σ_{π=0} λ D̂ that no residual corrects: at most 6e-4, i.e. ≤ 6 % of ε = 0.01. Fixed:
  identical strings share one noise draw (`110_unit_audit.py --syn_noise string`, default; `output` reproduces the old
  records bit-for-bit) and the noise scale is set on the non-identical pairs. Re-run of the four MT dial cells: pilot ρ
  within 0.03 of target; corr(pred, real) 0.975 → 0.971, realised HES at ρ = 0.5 / 0.8 11 % / 32 % → 10 % / 31 %;
  per-cell changes are Monte-Carlo sized (mean −0.5 points, random sign). Arena cells are unaffected (no identical outputs).
* Real evaluators: 13–14 of the 31 WMT22 metrics give identical strings slightly different scores (neural metrics; MATESE
  on zh→en by up to 5 points in 0.15 % of groups), but the resulting term is at most 7e-6. The estimator now states
  D̂ = 0 where outputs coincide.
* "Oracle" coefficient: the population least-squares coefficient. The coefficient minimising the realised Poisson-design
  variance (weights (1−π)/π) exceeds it in variance reduction by a median 0.0 %, 90th percentile 0.2 %, max 3.7 % (31
  metrics + three synthetic evaluators, 20 pilots, weighted design, top-4 menus). ρ²(1−P/J) is worded as an
  interpretive approximation throughout; "at most" removed from the conclusion.
