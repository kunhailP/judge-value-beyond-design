\begin{abstract}
LLM judges are increasingly credited with the human annotation they save. That credit is usually measured against uniform
annotation: label a random subset, then correct the judge's bias with the labels. We measure it instead against the
cheapest annotation design that uses no judge, for a concrete decision: certifying that a selected system is within
$\eps$ of the best of a menu. We define a judge's value as the fraction of human labels it saves for that decision
(HES), and measure it in three domains: retrieval (nine fully judged collections), machine translation (WMT22 MQM) and
open-ended chat (LMArena), with eleven judges, pre-registered hypotheses and a held-out locked test.
\res{Abstract numbers: (i) decision-aware human designs save X--Y\%; judges add A--B\% on top; (ii) inflation; (iii)
pair-conditional value, accuracy vs $\rho$; (iv) pilot predicts relative cost, not near-threshold adoption.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Human evaluation is the reference against which NLP systems are compared, and it is expensive. LLM judges promise to
stretch it: label everything with the judge, and spend human effort only on correcting the judge's bias
\citep{angelopoulos2023ppi,angelopoulos2023ppipp,boyeau2024autoeval,chen2026noisy}. A growing line of work reports how
much this saves, as an effective sample size or a ratio of human labels \citep{boyeau2024autoeval,gao2026ppsr,dorner2025limits}.

These savings are almost always computed against a single baseline: humans label a uniformly random subset of
the evaluation items. But uniform sampling is rarely the best use of a human budget. When the question is a decision,
\emph{is system $A$ within $\eps$ of the best alternative?}, many items cannot move the answer (two systems return the
same output, or a document lies outside both systems' cutoffs), and others move it a lot. An annotation design that
knows this, with no judge at all, can need far fewer labels. A judge's saving over uniform sampling therefore mixes two
things: what the judge contributes, and what any decision-aware design would have contributed anyway.

We separate the two. For a decision $d$ (a menu of systems, a tolerance $\eps$, a confidence level), an annotation
design $s$ and a judge $f$, we measure $\J(s,f;d)$, the number of human labels, pilot included, at which the decision is
certified with probability $\tau$, and define the judge's \emph{human-evidence saving}
$\HES_s(f;d)=1-\J(s,f;d)/\J(s,\emptyset;d)$. We report HES over the best judge-free design rather than over uniform
sampling, and the difference between the two, which we call \emph{inflation}.

We introduce no estimator: the corrected estimator is PPI++ \citep{angelopoulos2023ppipp} and the allocation is
active statistical inference \citep{zrnic2024active} for the functional the decision requires. The contribution is the
measurement target (labels to a decision, against the best judge-free design), its evaluation across three NLP
evaluation settings under pre-registration, and what it shows.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Decision.} A menu $\mathcal M$ of systems is evaluated on a fixed set of $N$ items with human utilities
$u_m(i)$; $\mu_m$ is the mean over the set. An audit selects a candidate $\hat m$ on a pilot and \emph{certifies} it if,
for every competitor $j$, a one-sided upper confidence bound on $\mu_j-\mu_{\hat m}$ is at most $\eps$ (Bonferroni over
competitors, $\alpha=0.10$). A certificate is \emph{wrong} if $\max_j\mu_j-\mu_{\hat m}>\eps$.

\paragraph{Designs.} After a fully labelled pilot of $P$ items, human labels are drawn by Poisson sampling with inclusion
probabilities $\pi_i$; the paired difference $D_j=u_j-u_{\hat m}$ is estimated by
\begin{equation}
\hat\Delta_j=\tfrac1N\Big[\sum_{i\in\text{pilot}}D_j(i)+\sum_{i\notin\text{pilot}}\lambda_j\hat D_j(i)+\sum_{i\in S}\tfrac{D_j(i)-\lambda_j\hat D_j(i)}{\pi_i}\Big],
\end{equation}
where $\hat D_j$ is the judge's predicted difference and $\lambda_j\ge0$ is fitted on the pilot and then frozen
($\lambda=0$: humans only). The estimator is unbiased for any judge given the pilot; its variance is estimated by
$\sum_{S}(1-\pi_i)R_i^2/\pi_i^2/N^2$ with $R=D-\lambda\hat D$. \emph{Uniform} designs use constant $\pi$ and label every
system's output of a sampled item. \emph{Decision-aware} designs use the structure of the decision: they never label an
item whose difference is known to be zero (identical outputs; documents outside every cutoff), label a shared output
once for all comparisons, and sample the rest in proportion to a label-free proxy of $|D|$ (closed-form decision
weights in retrieval, output dissimilarity in MT). \emph{Active} designs replace the proxy by the pilot-estimated
residual variance of the judge \citep{zrnic2024active}.

\paragraph{Cost and metric.} Cost counts unique human labels, pilot included. Each budget is a separate pre-committed
audit; $\J$ interpolates the certification rate over budgets ($\tau=0.5$ primary, $0.8$ where reached). HES, inflation
and selection regret are defined in the introduction and Section~\ref{sec:select}; intervals are paired bootstraps over
audits.

\section{Domains, judges and protocol}\label{sec:domains}
\paragraph{Retrieval.} Policies are three cutoff rules over a shared ranked pool \res{(describe)}; four development
collections (ANTIQUE, CAsT 2019, DBpedia-Entity, TREC DL 21--23), two collections used for a locked rule test
(TREC-COVID, Touché) and one never used in development (NeuCLIRBench). Judges: Qwen3-8B (UMBRELA prompt), Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B,
the inverted reranker (adversarial).
\paragraph{Machine translation.} WMT22 MQM \citep{freitag2021mqm} en$\to$de (1{,}315 segments) and zh$\to$en (1{,}875),
the top four WMT submissions by human MQM. Judges: COMET-22 \citep{rei2022comet}, Qwen3-8B and Mistral-7B with the
GEMBA-DA prompt \citep{kocmi2023gemba}, chrF (development), inverted COMET (adversarial).
\paragraph{Chat.} LMArena human-preference battles \citep{chiang2024arena}, the six model pairs with the most decisive
English single-turn battles (494--922 per pair). Judges: Qwen3-8B and Mistral-7B as pairwise judges (both orders
averaged), ``the longer response wins'', and inverted Qwen3-8B.
\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Hypotheses H1--H5 for MT and chat were committed before any LLM or COMET judgment was
joined with a human label (Appendix~\res{X}); the retrieval held-out test followed an earlier lock.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Judges as system rankers.} JuStRank \citep{gera2025justrank} and \citet{gao2025reeval} show that instance-level
judge accuracy does not determine system-level ranking quality; \citet{zhang2026utility} show that judgment quality and
downstream utility need not align. We move the target one step further, to the human labels a decision requires.
\paragraph{Prediction-powered evaluation.} PPI and PPI++ \citep{angelopoulos2023ppi,angelopoulos2023ppipp}, active
inference \citep{zrnic2024active}, AutoEval \citep{boyeau2024autoeval} and prediction-powered ranking \citep{chatzi2024ppr}
on Chatbot Arena, efficient noisy-judge inference \citep{chen2026noisy}, PRECISE for ranking metrics \citep{divekar2026precise},
and PPSR, which ranks MT metrics by annotation saved \citep{gao2026ppsr}. All of these measure savings over uniform labelling;
\citet{dorner2025limits} bound them by a factor of two. We measure them over the best judge-free design.
\paragraph{Annotation design for evaluation.} Active comparison of models \citep{sawade2012active}, active testing
\citep{kossen2021active}, topic-set design and active judgment selection in IR \citep{sakai2016topic,li2017active}.
